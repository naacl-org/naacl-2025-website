## Visas

The following questions will help you determine whether and how to apply for a U.S. visa to attend the conference.

**Can I travel to the U.S. without a visa?**  
Please see [this page](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/tourism-visit/visa-waiver-program.html) to determine whether you are eligible to apply for an [Electronic System Travel Authorization (ESTA](https://esta.cbp.dhs.gov/) instead, which might be cheaper and easier. This is usually allowed if you hold a passport from a country that participates in the Visa Waiver Program, but there are some exceptions. Be sure to apply for the ESTA at least 72 hours in advance. [Do not purchase your ESTA from any website other than the one linked above.](https://visaguide.world/tips/how-to-avoid-esta-fraud/)

**What kind of visa do I need?**  
To attend the conference, you should apply for a nonimmigrant B-1 visa. (This also [permits](https://www.cbp.gov/sites/default/files/documents/B-1%20permissible%20activities.pdf) you to engage in tourism during your trip.)

**How do I apply for a visa?**  
Please apply well in advance. Visa applications can take from 1–4 months to be approved and normally require an interview at a U.S. Embassy or Consulate. For details, begin [here](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/tourism-visit/visitor.html) and navigate to the website for the embassy or consulate nearest you for further visa instructions.

**How much will it cost?**  
The non-refundable visa application fee is [approximately $185](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/fees/fees-visa-services.html#temp) in local currency. Depending on your nationality, you may also have to pay a [visa issuance fee](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/Visa-Reciprocity-and-Civil-Documents-by-Country.html).

## About Albuquerque

Albuquerque, New Mexico, is the state’s largest city, its economic capital, and is home to over a half million people in the city proper. Its metropolitan area comprises four counties: Bernalillo, Sandoval, Torrance and Valencia to the south, home to nearly a million people inhabiting 9 square miles. It is a city with a long, rich, multicultural history that began long before its founding as a Spanish colony in 1706. Read about Albuquerque’s culture and history, things to do, and what it would be like to live in this vibrant city. Learn more here: [Greetings from Albuquerque!](https://albuquerque.com/?gad_source=1)

### Things to do in Albuquerque

Albuquerque, New Mexico, offers a variety of activities that cater to diverse interests. Start your visit by exploring the historic Old Town, where you can wander through charming streets lined with adobe buildings, shops, and restaurants. For a breathtaking experience, ride the Sandia Peak Tramway and enjoy stunning views from the top of the Sandia Mountains. If you're an early riser, consider a hot air balloon ride to see the city from above. Learn about local culture at the Indian Pueblo Cultural Center and the Turquoise Museum, where you can delve into the history and significance of turquoise. Art enthusiasts will appreciate the Albuquerque Museum of Art and History, while nature lovers can relax at the Albuquerque Botanic Garden. Savor the flavors of New Mexican cuisine at local eateries, and check out the local events calendar for festivals and performances during your stay. All these activities combined will ensure a memorable visit to Albuquerque. Enjoy your time in Albuquerque! https://albuquerque.com/things-to-do/
